# Client brief

Example brief for The Marketing Room. Ardentry, its product, its competitors and its customers are fictional.

## Client and offer (critical)
Ardentry, Inc. (fictional), a B2B SaaS company headquartered in Chicago with a sales office in London. Its product, Ardentry Close, is workflow software for mid-market finance teams: month-end close checklists, account reconciliations, journal entry approvals and audit trails, with integrations to the common mid-market ERPs. Target customers have 200 to 2,000 employees. Annual contracts start at 18,000 USD; average first-year contract value is about 32,000 USD. The offer for this campaign is a 30-minute demo, supported by a gated "Month-End Close Checklist" PDF for prospects who are not ready to talk to sales.

## Work type (critical)
campaign

## Business goal (critical)
Pipeline. In the CMO's words: "We need a predictable pipeline engine going into Q1 so sales is not living off referrals and events."

## Objective and KPIs (critical)
- Primary: 400 MQLs per month at or under 150 USD CPL (media cost divided by MQLs).
- Primary: 60 SQLs per month.
- Whether November counts as a ramp month or must hit the full targets: unknown.
- MQL definition (set in HubSpot by marketing ops): a demo request or checklist download from a business email address, with a finance or accounting job title, at a company of 200 to 2,000 employees.
- SQL definition: an MQL accepted by an account executive after a discovery call and created as a HubSpot deal.
- Historic MQL-to-SQL rate: about 15% (client estimate, not from a report).
- Secondary: demo requests, cost per SQL, organic sessions to product pages.

## Budget (critical)
60,000 USD per month for media, November 2026 to January 2027, so about 180,000 USD across the flight. Agency fees are excluded. Production and the landing page rebuild are not paid from the media budget; whether a separate production budget exists: unknown.

## Timeline (critical)
- Today: 2026-10-01
- Launch: 2026-11-02
- Flight end: 2027-01-29
- CMO sign-off on the plan wanted by 2026-10-14, so claims can go to legal in time for launch.
- Legal review of claims: 5 working days per submission.
- US Thanksgiving: 2026-11-26. Expect low B2B activity from 2026-11-25 to 2026-11-27.
- Year-end holiday slowdown in the US and UK: roughly 2026-12-21 to 2027-01-04.
- Many target customers close their fiscal year on 2026-12-31, so finance teams are busy with year-end close through mid-January.
- Mid-flight review with the CMO: week of 2026-12-07.

## Geography and language (critical)
United States and United Kingdom. English only, with US spelling for US ads and pages and UK spelling for UK. Sales coverage is 6 account executives in the US and 2 in the UK. No budget split between markets has been set; the CMO said "the UK has to pay its way".

## Audience
- Economic buyer: CFO or VP Finance.
- Champion and daily user: Controller, Assistant Controller, Accounting Manager, Finance Operations lead.
- Companies of 200 to 2,000 employees; best fit so far in SaaS, professional services, healthcare services and multi-entity groups.
- Pain in their words, from sales call notes: "our close takes ten-plus days", "reconciliations live in spreadsheets", "audit prep is a fire drill".
- They usually evaluate 2 or 3 vendors. Sales cycle is 60 to 90 days.

## Channels in scope and out of scope
- In scope: Google Ads (Search now; other campaign types open for recommendation), LinkedIn Ads, Meta Ads, SEO, and a rebuild of the demo landing page.
- Out of scope: TikTok, programmatic display, YouTube, events, outbound SDR programmes.
- Email nurture runs in HubSpot and stays in-house. The agency may recommend changes but will not build flows.
- Tried before: Google Search from March to August 2026; LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms in Q4 2025. Meta has never been run.

## Current state
- GA4 installed through Google Tag Manager. Form fills are tracked as a GA4 key event.
- Google Ads: active account, Search only. The conversion action is the GA4 form-fill key event imported into Google Ads. Enhanced Conversions not set up.
- LinkedIn: Campaign Manager account with the Insight Tag installed; conversions come from the Insight Tag and Lead Gen Forms.
- Meta: Business Manager and a browser pixel on the site. No Conversions API.
- CRM: HubSpot holds leads, lifecycle stages and deals. No offline conversion import to any ad platform, so every platform optimises to form fills, not to MQLs or SQLs.
- Consent: no banner in the US. The UK site shows a cookie banner but has no consent mode; whether tags wait for consent: unknown. CMP vendor: unknown.
- Past performance (the client's figures from an email, not an export):
  - Google Search, March to August 2026: about 12,000 USD per month, about 95 form fills per month, about 126 USD per form fill, with roughly 40% of spend on brand terms. Share of those form fills that became MQLs: unknown.
  - LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms, Q4 2025: 25,000 USD in total, 119 leads, about 210 USD per lead. Sales rated most of them poor quality (students, consultants, companies under 50 employees).

## Assets and resources
- Brand guidelines: yes (version 3, 2025), covering logo, colour, type and tone of voice.
- Existing creative: product screenshots, two approved customer case studies (a 180-person SaaS company and a 900-person healthcare group, both fictional), and a 60-second product walkthrough video.
- Landing pages: the current demo page is a generic 11-field contact form on the main site, converting at about 2.1% from paid search (client estimate). It is being rebuilt as part of this work.
- CMS: Webflow.
- Developer: one in-house developer, 2 days a week, who also owns site maintenance.
- Design: one in-house brand designer at about half time on this campaign. No in-house video production.
- Content: a freelance writer delivers two blog posts a month.

## Competitors
All fictional:
- Reconzo: the category leader in mid-market close software. Heavy on LinkedIn and paid search, bids on category terms.
- Tallyhawk: cheaper, aimed at companies under 300 employees.
- PeriodPilot: an ERP add-on sold through ERP resellers.
- The real alternative for most prospects: spreadsheets plus the close features built into their ERP.

## Mandatories and constraints
- No bidding on competitor brand terms, and no competitor names in ad copy or on landing pages.
- Every claim (for example "close five days faster") needs substantiation and legal review. Legal takes 5 working days per submission and prefers all claims in one batch.
- Customer names and logos may only come from the two approved case studies.
- UK: GDPR and PECR apply. Consent handling must be fixed before UK remarketing or conversion tracking depends on it.
- Brand: write "Ardentry Close" in full on first mention; no stock photos of people smiling at laptops.

## Approvals and stakeholders
- Sign-off: the CMO approves the plan, the budget split and final creative.
- Day to day: the Head of Demand Generation.
- Legal: in-house counsel reviews every claim (5 working days).
- Sales: the VP Sales must agree the MQL and SQL definitions and lead routing.
- The CMO reviews documents on Wednesdays; allow one week for plan approval.

## Data files and links
None supplied. The past performance figures above come from the client's email, not from an export.

## Deliverable format
One integrated 90-day plan the CMO can approve in a single review: budget by channel and month, the forecast against the KPIs, tracking fixes needed before launch, the landing page plan, the creative plan and a week-by-week timeline. Markdown document.

## Anything else
- The CMO is sceptical of LinkedIn after the Q4 2025 test and wants it justified with numbers, not dropped by default.
- Sales capacity: the account executives can run about 80 discovery calls a month in total.
- The developer's two days are shared; site maintenance comes first if something breaks.
