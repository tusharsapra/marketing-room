# Work type: Campaign

## What the client is buying
A launch-ready media plan that turns the budget into a channel split, a flighting schedule and a forecast the client can hold the agency to. Every channel has to connect to the business goal through tracking that works on day one, creative that fits each placement, and landing pages that convert the traffic being bought. The client should be able to approve it, brief their teams from it and start spending without another planning round.

## What intake must pull out of the brief
- The business goal and the single primary KPI, with its target, unit and period (for example 400 MQLs per month at or under 150 USD CPL), plus any secondary KPIs.
- The definition of the conversion that counts (lead, MQL, SQL, purchase, install) and where it is recorded: ad platform, GA4, CRM or store.
- Budget amount, currency and period (per month, per flight or total), and whether agency fees, production and tools come out of it. If no budget is given, record that and plan in percentages.
- Today's date, launch date, flight end, and the dates that change demand or capacity: holidays, sales events, product launches, the client's own busy periods.
- Markets and languages, and any stated split between markets.
- Channels in scope and out of scope, and any channel the client has tried before, with its result.
- Tracking status: conversion events defined, Enhanced Conversions, CAPI, consent mode and CMP per market, offline conversion import from the CRM.
- Past performance that can anchor a forecast (CPL, CPA, ROAS, CTR, conversion rate by channel), with the date range it covers.
- Production and build capacity: who makes creative, how fast, and how many developer days exist for landing pages and tagging.
- Lead times that constrain the timeline: legal or claims review, brand approval, account setup, and who signs off the plan.

## What each seat's kickoff should focus on
- Ad seats (google-ads, meta-ads, linkedin-ads and any bench ad seat cast): the campaign structure, the budget requested with the maths behind it (expected CPC or CPM, conversion rate, cost per result), the minimum budget below which the channel should not run, the learning or ramp period, and the creative volume needed per campaign or ad set.
- measurement: the conversion events and their definitions, the gap between the brief's KPI and what the platforms can see today, which fixes must be live before launch and which can follow, and the reporting cadence.
- client-lead: a first view of the budget split and its reasoning, the KPIs the client will actually judge the agency on, and the approvals and dates that constrain the plan.
- seo: what organic search can deliver inside the flight versus after it, keyword overlap with paid search, and technical fixes that also help paid landing pages.
- ux, ui and developer: the landing-page journey per audience or offer, the build scope, and a realistic live date for each page given the stated developer capacity.
- creative-strategist, copywriter and creative-ops: the core message and angles per audience, message match from ad to page, the asset count by channel and format, and production lead times including claims review.
- Usually essential: client-lead, measurement, every ad seat for an in-scope channel, creative-strategist and creative-ops. Cast ux and developer when landing pages are in scope, seo when organic search is in scope, and copywriter when copy or claims review is part of the work.

## Deliverable outline
1. Executive summary: the goal, the recommended split in one line, the forecast against the KPI target, the three things that must be true for the plan to work, and the decisions needed from the client.
2. Objectives and KPIs: the primary KPI with target and period, secondary KPIs, conversion definitions, and how each KPI maps to a platform, GA4 or CRM metric.
3. Audience and targeting: the audiences per channel, how each is built (keywords, lists, interests, firmographics, lookalikes), and the split between markets.
4. Budget and channel split: a table of budget by channel and month in the brief's currency with share of total, forecast volume and cost per result per channel, the maths behind each forecast, and what is in or out of the budget (fees, production, tools).
5. Channel plans: one subsection per channel with campaign structure, bidding, targeting, creative needs, learning period and the optimisation routine for the flight.
6. Creative plan: the proposition and angles, the asset list by channel and format, the testing plan and refresh cadence, and the date each batch is ready after review.
7. Landing pages and conversion path: each page with its audience and offer, the form or checkout, the build scope and owner, and the live date against the launch date.
8. Measurement plan: conversion events, tagging and server-side fixes with deadlines, consent handling per market, CRM or offline import, attribution approach, dashboards and reporting cadence.
9. Timeline and phasing: a week-by-week or month-by-month schedule from today to flight end covering setup, launch, learning, optimisation and scaling, with budget phasing and key dates.
10. Owners and responsibilities: a table of each workstream with the accountable seat, the client-side owner and the due date.
11. Risks and mitigations: each risk with likelihood, impact, early warning sign, mitigation and owner.
12. Assumptions: every assumption the numbers depend on, its source (brief, client data, benchmark or judgement) and how it will be checked.
13. Decisions and open questions for the client: each decision needed with options, our recommendation and a needed-by date, followed by open questions.

## Acceptance criteria
- Channel budgets add up to the stated budget for each period, in the brief's currency, with fees and production treated as the brief says.
- Every forecast figure shows its inputs (CPC or CPM, conversion rate, cost per result), and each input traces to the brief, the client's data or a named benchmark.
- The executive summary compares the forecast with the KPI target and states any shortfall plainly, with what would close it.
- No channel is budgeted below its platform minimum or below the volume it needs to exit learning, unless the plan says why it accepts that.
- Every in-scope channel has a channel plan, and no out-of-scope channel receives budget.
- The timeline starts from today's date, allows for legal, build and approval lead times, and shows every pre-launch tracking fix finishing before launch.
- The measurement plan names each conversion event, where it is recorded and how each platform receives it, including consent handling in every market with privacy rules.
- Every workstream in the owners table has exactly one accountable seat and a due date.
- The asset count in the creative plan matches what the channel plans say they need, and production dates land before each launch or refresh date.
- Every client decision has a recommendation and a needed-by date.
